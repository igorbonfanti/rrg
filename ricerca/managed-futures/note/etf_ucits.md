Research date: 17 October 2026. Most figures come from justETF (Italian site) and other aggregators, read on or around this date. justETF does not give an as-of date for fund size, so AUM figures are "latest shown on justETF, Oct 2026". Where sources conflict, both are given.

## 1. iMGP DBi Managed Futures Fund UCITS ETF (the UCITS version of DBMF): product table

### Takeaway
The DBi strategy exists in Europe as an active UCITS ETF share class of the Luxembourg SICAV sub-fund "iMGP DBi Managed Futures Fund". It has three ETF classes, all accumulating with a 0.75% TER: USD unhedged (LU2951555585, ticker DBMF), EUR-denominated unhedged (LU2951555403, DBMFE) and EUR hedged (LU3359622902, MFEH, launched 13 May 2026). There is also an "ex-Commodities" sister fund (LU3024069877, MFA8). **None of them is listed on Borsa Italiana.** The venues are Euronext Paris, Xetra and LSE, so an Italian investor buys them through a broker with access to foreign exchanges (e.g. Fineco, which publishes an Italian KID).

### Methodology (DBi replication)
- DBi aims to replicate the pre-fee returns of the ~20 large CTAs in the SG CTA Index. A 2024 HedgeNordic description says it uses about four weeks of average daily net returns (adjusted to pre-fee), then quadratic optimisation to estimate the aggregate long/short positions in about ten major futures contracts. Earlier ETF Trends/ETFdb descriptions mention a trailing 60-day window and about 10-15 contracts. — [HedgeNordic via MFN "Pros and cons of replicating all-stars trend followers"](https://mfn.se/a/hedgenordic/pros-and-cons-of-replicating-all-stars-trend-followers); [ETF Trends "DBi talks single fund replication"](https://www.etftrends.com/managed-futures-channel/dbi-talks-single-fund-replication/); [ETFdb](https://etfdb.com/managed-futures-content-hub/dbi-eyeing-single-fund-replication/)
- The rebalancing frequency is not stated in any primary iMGP document I could read. A similar, rules-based US product (Simplify SDMF index, launched Feb 2026) rebuilds weekly across ten liquid futures. That is a different product and does not confirm DBi's own cadence. — [Simplify SDMF factsheet](https://www.simplify.us/sites/default/files/etfs/factsheet/2026-02/SDMF-Fact-Sheet.pdf)
- DBi's framework is also licensed to the Fortem Capital Managed Futures Fund (a UCITS fund, not an ETF, launched June 2026, for professional and institutional investors). — [Funds Europe](https://funds-europe.com/fortem-launches-low-cost-managed-futures-ucits-fund/)

### Inferences
- The UCITS ETF classes add up to roughly EUR 385m (158 + 204 + 23, justETF). The whole sub-fund, including non-ETF classes, was EUR 657m per finanzfluss. Adding the ex-commodities fund (~EUR 323m) puts the iMGP DBi UCITS franchise at roughly EUR 1bn, by far the largest managed-futures ETF franchise in Europe.
- The USD and EUR-unhedged classes report almost identical YTD returns (+24.2% vs +24.9%). This fits the same USD portfolio with a small EUR/USD move. The EUR-unhedged class therefore carries full EUR/USD risk: it is a different quote currency, not a hedge.
- The UCITS ETF charges 0.75% against 0.85% for the US ETF. Pre-fee, the two should behave very similarly, but no published tracking-difference figure between UCITS DBMF and US DBMF (or vs the SG CTA Index) was found.
- The ex-Commodities version is not authorised for marketing in Italy (per justETF). An Italian retail investor may be unable to buy it, or could buy it only on an execution-only basis.

### Gaps
- No official iMGP factsheet or KID text could be read: imgp.com pages returned only navigation, and the Fineco KID PDF could not be parsed. TER, transaction costs, SRI and exact AUM dates are therefore aggregator-sourced.
- No tracking-difference data for UCITS DBMF vs US DBMF or vs the SG CTA Index (or SG Trend Index) since launch was found.
- The calendar-2025 return of the UCITS classes is not shown by justETF.
- No GBP-hedged or CHF-hedged class was found, and none has been ruled out.
- The exact rebalancing frequency is not confirmed from primary sources.

## 4. EUR-hedged share class and the cost/benefit of hedging in 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Yes, an EUR-hedged class exists: iMGP DBi Managed Futures Fund R EUR HP UCITS ETF (LU3359622902, MFEH), launched 13 May 2026 on Euronext Paris and Xetra. It is **not listed in Milan**, and it is small (EUR 23m). With the Fed at 3.75-4.00% and the ECB deposit rate at 2.50% after the September 2026 hikes (lower-quality source), hedging USD to EUR costs roughly 1.25-1.5 percentage points a year of carry. In exchange it removes EUR/USD exposure. The direction of the EUR/USD effect in 2026 is not clear from the data found: over 3 months to mid-October, the hedged class returned +13.27% (justETF) against +10.40% for the EUR-unhedged class (finanzfluss). That would imply the USD weakened vs EUR in that window, but the two figures come from different sources and dates.

### Inferences
- A managed-futures fund holds most of its assets in short-term T-bills as collateral. The USD class earns USD cash rates plus the trend return; the EUR-hedged class roughly earns EUR cash rates plus the trend return. The hedge drag is therefore about the short-rate differential, ~1.25-1.5% a year at October 2026 rates (less if the ECB hikes again). Over the last 3 months, the hedged class returned +13.27% (justETF) against +10.40% for the EUR-unhedged class (finanzfluss, to 15 Oct 2026). Over that window, FX moves outweighed the carry drag. Windows and sources differ, so this is not a clean comparison.
- The unhedged classes (DBMF USD or DBMFE EUR) add USD exposure to the trend return. The USD has historically tended to strengthen in risk-off episodes, which adds to the crisis-alpha profile. The hedged class isolates the pure strategy return for a EUR investor, at a cost of about 1.3-1.5% a year now. The hedged class is also much smaller and newer, so it may have wider spreads.
